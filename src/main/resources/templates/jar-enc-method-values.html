Reading this

The grids are the page. The two lists advertise every pair between them, and each client may use one. A client reading discovery and choosing freely from both lists has three ways out of four to be wrong, and the wrongness is not about cryptography — every cell here is a well-formed JWE this server could open.

One client never registered a method at all. pkce-demo-client registered neither half, and still has a pair: OpenID Connect Registration §2 says the method defaults to A128CBC-HS256 when an algorithm is registered — which that client did not do either, so both halves of its pair come from this server reading silence as the defaults rather than as permission. Its accepted cell is the top left, and nothing about that is visible in the lists.

The refusals say which half was wrong. A cell marked wrong alg was refused before the content encryption mattered; one marked wrong enc got past the algorithm and failed on the method. Two checks in sequence, and a client debugging from a single "invalid_request_object" would not know which of its two registrations to look at.

Off the list is a third kind of no. The last row sends A192CBC-HS384 from the client that registered it — so it is not a mismatch. It is refused because the method is on neither list, which is what the method page shows from the registration's side and this one shows from the document's.

That completes RFC 9101 §4's three lists. Signing algorithms, encryption algorithms and the methods here are all published, all derived from the constants the filter enforces, and all three were either absent or wrong a few rounds ago.
